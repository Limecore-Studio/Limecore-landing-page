Privacy Policy

Last updated 18 November 2026 · covers Nexus Command Center, LimeLog and StudyDesk

This policy covers all three Limecore apps together, because they share one account system and one database. It describes what the apps actually do — if anything here does not match the software, that is a bug, and we want to hear about it.

The short version.
  • Without an account, nothing you enter leaves your device. All three apps work fully offline as a guest.
  • With an account, your data syncs so you can use more than one device and survive changing phone.
  • No advertising, and nothing that follows you around. The Android apps contain no third-party analytics code at all — not disabled, not present. The two websites count page views without cookies and without anything tied to your account; that is described in full below.
  • Error reports are off until you switch them on. If an app crashes and you have allowed it, it sends us a technical description of the failure, never the things you entered. They are erased after 90 days.
  • Two small things are also off until you say yes: a note of the days you open each app, and the Android apps' daily check for updates on F-Droid. Each app asks once, and each has a switch in Settings.
  • The AI features are off until you switch them on in Settings, in every app. If you do switch them on, what you send may be used by Google to train its models — we run on their free tier. Details below.
  • You can export everything and delete your account and all its data from inside any of the three apps, without asking us.
  • One account works across all three apps. Deleting it deletes everything, in all three.

Guest mode

Every app can be used without an account. In guest mode everything you enter — your finances, training, coursework, tasks and habits — is written to your device's local storage and nowhere else. We never receive it, cannot read it, and cannot recover it for you. Uninstalling the app deletes it.

One honest caveat: Nexus Command Center still fetches public market prices and exchange rates while you are a guest, because a price chart with no prices is not much use. Those requests carry a ticker symbol, never anything you entered. See Who else sees your data.

If you say yes, the Android apps also ask f-droid.org, at most once a day, whether a newer version exists, so they can tell you about fixes you have not installed. That request names the app and nothing else. It is off until you answer: each app asks once, a guest included, and you can change your answer in Settings. Error reports, feedback and the app-open record need an account, so a guest never sends any of them.

What is stored when you sign in

Your data lives in a Supabase database hosted in Stockholm, Sweden (EU). Every table enforces row-level security, so the database itself — not just the app code — prevents anyone from reading rows that are not theirs.

Opening it again the same day changes nothing. It records that you came back, not what you did — no screens, no times, nothing you entered. We use it to see whether people return to the apps, which tells us whether they are worth building on. Kept for 13 months, then deleted automatically; deleting your account deletes it at once. It is included in your data export, and you can ask us to erase it sooner.

It is off until you say yes. Each app asks once for each account, and each has a switch in Settings to turn it off again. Turning it off stops new rows from being recorded; it does not remove the days already recorded, so ask us if you want those gone too.

What we never collect

No location, no contacts, no advertising or device identifiers, no browsing history. The apps request no permissions for any of it. (Error reports and the app-open record, described above, are off unless you say yes. Error reports are about crashes rather than about you; the app-open record holds a date, an app version and a platform, and nothing about what you did.)

The only sensitive permissions any of the three ever ask for are notifications, the camera (LimeLog progress photos, which stay on your device) and Health Connect (LimeLog steps and calories, which are read and discarded). Each is requested only when you use the feature that needs it, each can be declined, and declining costs you that one feature and nothing else.

All three Android apps also use the phone's vibration motor for a short buzz when you tap certain buttons. That permission only lets an app vibrate; it reads nothing and sends nothing.

Who else sees your data

We do not sell your data, ever, to anyone, for any purpose. The services below are the only ones that receive anything at all, and each receives only what this section says.

Supabase

Hosts the database and the private file storage, and handles sign-in. Data is stored in the EU (Stockholm, Sweden).

Resend (email)

Sends the emails the apps need: sign-in links, confirmation and email-change messages, and the code that confirms a second Ko-fi address. Resend receives the address it sends to and the message, and nothing else from your account.

hCaptcha (sign-up and sign-in check)

When you create an account or sign in, the apps may show an hCaptcha check (run by Intuition Machines, Inc.) to stop automated sign-ups. It is loaded only on that screen, and it sees your IP address and browser or device details while it runs. It is not used anywhere else in the apps.

GitHub (desktop updates)

The Windows editions of Nexus Command Center and StudyDesk check GitHub for a newer version and download updates from it. Like any web request, GitHub sees the IP address the check comes from; the request says nothing about you or your data.

Google

Only in two situations, both of which you choose:

  • "Continue with Google" sign-in. Google tells us your email address, name and profile picture. Your Google account itself is governed by Google's privacy policy.
  • The AI features, described in full below.

Market data providers (Nexus Command Center only)

To show prices, exchange rates and market indicators, Nexus Command Center requests public data from CoinGecko, Yahoo Finance, Finnhub, open.er-api.com, the European Central Bank, the US Federal Reserve, NYSE and CNN's market-indicator feed.

These requests carry a ticker symbol or a currency pair and nothing else — no account, no name, no email, no holdings, no amounts. They cannot see what you own or how much. They do, like any web request, see the IP address it came from. They happen whether or not you have an account, because prices are the same for everyone. The other two apps make no such requests.

Vercel and Google Fonts (the websites only)

Nexus Command Center and StudyDesk also run in a browser, at nexus.limecore.dev and studydesk.limecore.dev. Those two websites, and this site (limecore.dev), are hosted by Vercel. LimeLog has no web edition. Nothing in this section applies to the Android apps — the analytics code described here is excluded from them at build time, so it is not merely switched off in the APK, it is not in the file.

Page counting. The websites use Vercel Web Analytics, which records the page you opened, the site that linked you there, and a coarse country, browser and device type. It sets no cookies, stores nothing on your device, and is never joined to your account — we can see that a page was opened, not who opened it. We use it to know whether anyone is finding the apps at all. limecore.dev also uses Vercel Speed Insights, which measures how fast a page loaded, on the same terms: no cookies, nothing tied to you.

Fonts on limecore.dev. The pages of this site load their fonts from Google Fonts, so Google sees your IP address and browser details when you open one. The apps and the two app websites do not use Google Fonts.

Market data on the web edition takes a detour. A browser cannot call most of the market-data providers listed above directly, so on the Nexus Command Center website those requests are forwarded through Vercel first. Vercel therefore sees them, and may keep them in its request logs for a period it controls. The Android app calls the providers straight from your device and skips this entirely.

One consequence is worth stating plainly rather than leaving you to infer it. If you add your own Finnhub API key in Settings on the website, the key goes along with those forwarded requests. It travels in a hidden request header, not in the web address, so it is not part of the addresses Vercel keeps in its request logs. It does still pass through Vercel's servers on its way to Finnhub. If you would rather it did not, use the Android app, where the request never passes through us.

F-Droid (Android update check)

If you say yes, each Android app asks F-Droid's public website, at most once a day, which version it currently offers. The request names the app (for example com.StudyDesk.app) and carries nothing about you, your account or your data. Like any web request, F-Droid sees the IP address it came from. The check is off until you answer. Each app asks once, for each account or for a guest, and you can turn it on or off again in Settings. While it is off, the app never contacts F-Droid itself.

Ko-fi (only if you support the project)

All three apps have a "support this project" row in Settings. It is an ordinary link that opens ko-fi.com in your browser — there is no payment form inside the apps, no payment SDK, and no tracking pixel. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you tap it, and tapping it sends nothing about you beyond what any browser sends when it opens a page.

If you support the project, Ko-fi and the payment provider it shows you (PayPal or Stripe) handle the payment. We never see your card details — they do not pass through our apps or our database. Ko-fi and we are separate data controllers: what Ko-fi itself keeps is governed by Ko-fi's privacy policy.

What we receive and why. For a membership, Ko-fi tells us the email address the payment came from, the tier, and its own reference for the payment. We match that email address to your Limecore account so we can switch on the supporter theme and badge, the thank-you for supporting. We keep your tier, the dates your support started and runs until, the payment email and Ko-fi's reference, until you delete your account (or ask us to remove them). One-off tips switch nothing on and are not matched.

If you paid from a different address than the one you sign in with, you can confirm that address in Settings: we email it a one-time code through Resend, and once you enter the code we store the address with your account for the same purpose. A payment from an address no account matches yet is kept for 35 days, so it can be switched on when you confirm the address or sign up with it, and is then deleted whether or not it was claimed.

Every feature other than the supporter theme and badge is free either way.

The AI features

Each app has one optional feature that sends something to Google's Gemini model. All three are off until you switch them on in Settings, and they stay off after a fresh install, after a reinstall, and for anyone who signs in on a device where someone else turned them on.

StudyDesk — study debriefSends the note you type after a study session. Gemini returns the topic, a 1–5 comprehension rating, anything you flagged as confusing, and a one-line summary.
LimeLog — workout debriefSends the note you type after a session. Gemini returns an effort rating, any body parts you mentioned as sore, a mood, and a one-line summary.
Nexus Command Center — weekly summarySends your domain scores only — five numbers between 0 and 100, plus the pattern headlines already shown on the Life tab. It never sends transactions, amounts, account names, task text or habit names. Gemini returns two or three sentences.
Turning the switch on is how you consent, and turning it off is how you withdraw. While it is off, nothing whatsoever is sent to Gemini and every other feature works identically. While it is on, please do not write anything in those notes you would not want processed by a third party.

What is sent may be processed outside the EEA, under the Standard Contractual Clauses Google offers for international transfers. We do not send your email address, your name or your account ID along with it.
Read this before you switch it on. These apps use Google's free Gemini tier, and on that tier Google may use what is sent to improve its products, including training future versions of its models. That is the trade-off for the feature costing nothing to run, and we would rather say so plainly than bury it.

In practice this means: anything you type into a study or workout debrief could end up as training data at Google. The Nexus Command Center summary sends only five 0–100 scores and the headlines already on your screen, so there is nothing personal in it to train on — but the two debriefs send exactly the words you wrote.

If that is not acceptable to you, leave the switch off. Nothing else in any of the apps changes, and no feature other than these three is affected. If we ever move to Google's paid tier — where this use is contractually excluded — we will update this page and say so in the apps.

Why we are allowed to store it

  • To provide the app you asked for (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)) — your finances, training, coursework, tasks and habits, and the files you attach. Without them there is no app.
  • To give supporters what they paid for (Art. 6(1)(b)) — matching a Ko-fi payment to your account, and confirming a second Ko-fi address if you ask us to.
  • Your explicit consent (Art. 9(2)(a), with Art. 6(1)(a)) — health data in LimeLog: body measurements and notes about soreness or pain. You give it by answering yes to the one-time question, and withdraw it in Settings, which erases them from our server. See Health data.
  • Your consent (Art. 6(1)(a)) — the AI features. You give it by switching them on in Settings, and withdraw it by switching them off. To remove what was already sent, delete the sessions those notes belong to, or delete your account.
  • Your consent (Art. 6(1)(a)) also covers error reports, which you give by switching them on or sending a single report, and withdraw by switching them off; and feedback, which is sent only when you press Send.
  • Your consent (Art. 6(1)(a)) is also the basis for the app-open record and the F-Droid update check. You give it by answering yes when an app asks, or by switching them on in Settings, and withdraw it by switching them off. Withdrawing stops anything further from being sent. To remove the app-open days already recorded, email us or delete your account.

Age

You must be at least 16 to create an account, or the minimum age for consenting to online services where you live if that is lower — it is 13 in some EU countries.

StudyDesk in particular is used by people at school. If you are under that age, you can still use any of these apps: use them as a guest, where no account exists and nothing leaves your device. If you believe someone under that age has created an account, contact us and we will delete it.

How long we keep it

  • Until you delete it. We do not expire accounts or quietly remove old data.
  • When you delete an item in an app, it is marked deleted and hidden immediately, so the deletion reaches your other devices. Its content is erased automatically 90 days later. For StudyDesk items we then keep a marker with no content in it, just an identifier, the dates it was created, changed and deleted, and which course or note it belonged to, until you delete your account. Without that marker, a device you have not opened in a while could put the item back. For the other apps the deleted item is removed entirely after 90 days. Files attached to a deleted note or assignment are erased with it.
  • When you delete your account, everything is erased from the live database and file storage immediately and irreversibly, across all three apps. There is no recovery window.
  • Backups. Our database provider's backups roll off within 7 days. We also keep our own weekly backup of the database and the uploaded files, on our own computer, for up to 8 weeks. So something you deleted, or a deleted account, can remain in a backup for up to 8 weeks before it is gone for good. Backups are only ever used to restore the service after a failure.
  • Error reports are erased automatically 90 days after they are sent, or straight away if you delete your account.
  • The change history of Nexus Command Center tasks and budgets is erased after 90 days, and at once when the item is erased.
  • The app-open record is kept for 13 months, then deleted automatically; deleting your account deletes it at once.
  • Supporter status is kept until you delete your account or ask us to remove it. A Ko-fi payment no account matches is deleted after 35 days.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you may request access to your data, correct it, delete it, restrict or object to processing, and receive it in a portable format.

Two of those are buttons rather than requests, so you never have to wait for us:

  • Export my data — downloads everything we hold, as JSON, including feedback you sent and any error reports we still keep.
  • Delete my account — erases the account and all its data, across all three apps.

Both are in Settings, in all three apps. Because the three share one account, either button works from whichever app you happen to have open and covers your data in all of them — deleting from LimeLog erases your StudyDesk coursework too, and the confirmation says so before you commit.

Export also works as a guest. A guest's data never left the device, but it is still yours to take a copy of.

For anything else, email l1m3core@gmail.com.

If you think we have handled your data badly you can complain to the Finnish Data Protection Ombudsman (tietosuoja.fi), or to the supervisory authority where you live.

Security

Sign-in is handled by Supabase Auth; we never see or store your password. All traffic is HTTPS. Every table has row-level security enabled, enforced by the database rather than by application code, so a bug in an app cannot expose one user's rows to another.

No system is perfect. If we discover a breach affecting your personal data we will notify the Finnish Data Protection Ombudsman within 72 hours and tell you directly where the law requires it.
